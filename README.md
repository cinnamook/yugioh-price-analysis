# What explains the price of a Yu-Gi-Oh! card?

<!-- WORKING RULE #1: the question is written before any code. It is deliberately
     narrow — one cross-sectional question, not a survey of the dataset. -->

**The question, in one sentence:** Using a single snapshot of the YGOPRODeck database,
which observable attributes of a Yu-Gi-Oh! card — rarity of printing, age, ban-list
status, archetype, card type, and whether it has been reprinted — are associated with
its market price, and does ban-list status predict price once scarcity is accounted for?

**Framing (decided):** Unit of analysis is the **card** (~14k rows, one per card). We
originally chose printings so rarity could be a per-printing driver, but the data forced a
reversal: `set_price` is $0.00 (= no recorded sale) for **72%** of printings, and that
missingness is correlated with trading volume and therefore with **ban status** — which would
have biased the one finding we care most about. So price = card-level **TCGplayer** price
(96.5% coverage, independent rows, clean ban comparison), and rarity enters as a *card*
attribute (e.g. highest rarity the card was printed in) rather than a per-printing outcome.
The spine is **explanatory** — which attributes are associated with a card's price and by how
much, with ban-list status as the headline test — followed by a short **predictive coda**
(out-of-sample R²/RMSE on log-price) to show predictive power without making prediction the point.

## What this is not (stated up front, expanded in the Limitations section)

- A **snapshot**, not a time series. Nothing about price movement, ban-day crashes, or
  trends is answerable from this data.
- **Correlation only.** No causal claim that banning moves prices.
- Marketplace prices are **asks, not transactions** (verified per source in the notebook).
- **Survivorship:** cards still in print are not a random sample of cards ever printed.
